## Job Description (archived verbatim)

Applied AI Engineer

Location: Remote, Americas
Type: FullTime
Work model: Remote

About Monte Carlo

Monte Carlo is the agent trust platform that unifies data and agent observability to monitor, troubleshoot, and improve production AI systems. As enterprises prepare to deploy thousands of agents across business-critical use cases, Monte Carlo provides the reliability infrastructure to support them along this AI transformation, from human-guided agents to fully autonomous operations. Founded in 2019 and backed by leading investors, Monte Carlo empowers data and AI teams to ship trusted AI at scale.

THE ROLE

We're building the products that tell enterprises whether their AI agents can be trusted — and we need someone who works end to end, from an ambiguous problem statement through research, prototyping, and production. You'd get the problem, not the spec: research the approaches, prototype, prove what works, build it, and integrate it into the platform alongside our engineering and data science teams.

WHAT YOU'LL DO

- Take an open problem end-to-end — from research and prototyping through production.
- Design and ship agent-powered features — root-cause analysis, incident triage, monitor generation.
- Build the eval infrastructure that makes those features safe to change: golden datasets, regression suites, offline and online scoring.
- Own retrieval and context pipelines over customer metadata, lineage, and query history; instrument agent behavior in production — traces, failure taxonomies, cost and latency budgets.
- Partner with data science on detection quality and experiment design, and with PM on what an agent should do versus what it merely can do.
- Set the technical bar for how we build with LLMs — patterns, guardrails, and internal tooling.

WHAT WE'RE LOOKING FOR

- You've built agents in production with real users — agents with real autonomy and internal loops. RAG with a wrapper doesn't count. Neither does a set of MCP tools pointed at an API.
- You've run evals and monitored agents after launch — owned an eval framework: golden datasets, regression suites, offline and online scoring.
- Python, plus an ML or data science background.
- 3+ years in ML, data science, or software engineering — at companies that ship quickly.
- You work from a problem, not a spec.
- You use AI tools every day.
- You'd rather ship than polish.

Nice to have: statistics and hypothesis testing; building and maintaining MCP servers; experience in the data and cloud space — Snowflake, Databricks, dbt, Airflow.

THIS IS NOT FOR YOU IF

- Your AI work is retrieval with a wrapper, or MCP tools pointed at an API.
- Your LLM experience is prototypes, notebooks, and demos that never carried production traffic.
- You need a fully specified problem before you start.

### Free-text answers

1. **Describe one AI agent you personally built and shipped to production users. Be specific about what the agent decided without a human in the loop, which tools it could call, and roughly how many model calls a single user request involved.**

> I built and shipped a clinical decision-support agent at Sybrid that runs in a production clinical workflow. On each request it decides, without a human in the loop, which parts of the patient's history to retrieve and what medication or diagnostic guidance to generate from that context; a separate output-guardrail layer validates the recommendation before it is surfaced. Its tools were a retrieval layer over structured patient records (RAG on Node.js, PostgreSQL, and Redis) plus the LLM recommendation/diagnostic generator. A single request runs a retrieval pass, a generation call, and an output-validation step.

2. **Tell us about a problem you were given without a spec — no roadmap and nobody obvious to ask. What did you do first, what did you decide not to build, and what shipped?**

> At Sybrid I was handed an ambiguous goal — make clinical decision support reliable in production — with no spec, no roadmap, and nobody obvious to ask. I started by defining the retrieval, prompt, and output-guardrail architecture, then built the smallest end-to-end version and validated it against real patient-history retrieval. I deliberately prioritized retrieval grounding and output guardrails over adding breadth. What shipped was the production clinical decision-support agent, which generates medication recommendations and diagnostic guidance grounded in retrieved patient history.
